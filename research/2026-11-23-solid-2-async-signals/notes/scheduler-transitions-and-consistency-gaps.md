## 1. How `core/lanes.ts` manages concurrent world-views without duplicating the graph

### Takeaway

`core/lanes.ts` maintains concurrent world-views in-place using two value locations per node (`_value` for the screen, `_pendingValue` for staging, plus the `covers` record for unrevealed guesses) and routes each read in `laneRead` by reader posture (`CONFIG_AUTHORITATIVE`, untracked, `EFFECT_RENDER`, or tracked derivation) rather than cloning the reactive graph. Lanes are sub-`Transaction` objects (`_lane: true`, `_parent`) whose lifecycle is driven at flush seams (`laneSeams` / `laneSeam`), blocked together with linked lanes or their parent (`lanesBlocked`), and ended or rolled back via `dissolveLane` and `endLanes`.

### Cited findings

- "A lane is 'a new base of a transition': the sub-frame an optimistic write opens in the transition of the frame it is made in. It sees the screen plus its own guesses (plan sec. 19), breaks out of the parent's hold — its effects run now — and holds itself if its own derivations hit async (and, blocked, holds its parent)." ## 2. Core architectural layers and formal invariants in the 2.0 docs

### Takeaway

Solid 2.0's formal async specification organizes semantics into a strict three-tier rule hierarchy (`Fundamentals` `F-<n>` > `Derived rules` `D-<n>` > `Scenarios` `S-<n>`, over the frozen `A`/`O`/`V` archive) and implements them via the "L2 hold model" (which replaced the pre-L2 `_asyncReporters`/`_overrideValue`/`_firewall` design). Across `06-actions-optimistic.md`, `RULES-FUNDAMENTALS.md`, and `RULES-DERIVED.md`, the invariants enforce whole-frame visibility (`F-1`), hold-and-boundary scoping (`F-2`–`F-5`, `F-7`), display-ahead lane isolation (`F-6`), question-scoped verdicts (`F-9`), flush-atomicity (`F-10`), guess lifetime/provenance/reversion (`F-11`, `D-33`–`D-34`), and error-as-answer staging (`F-12`, `D-41`).
